How it works

  • 01 · rocket

    Your rocket carries a GPS transmitter (Eggfinder TX, Missile Works T3…) broadcasting its position by radio.

  • 02 · receiver

    Your handheld receiver picks up the RF signal and relays standard NMEA data to your phone over Bluetooth.

  • 03 · phone

    The app tracks the flight live, remembers the landing spot, and guides you straight to it — even with no cell signal.

Installing

The app installs directly from this page — no app store needed

  1. Download — tap the orange button above on your Android phone. Chrome may warn that the file “can harm your device”; that’s Android’s standard notice for any app from outside an app store. Tap Download anyway.
  2. Open the file — pull down the notification shade and tap the completed download (or find rocket-locator-26.apk in your Files app).
  3. Allow the install — the first time, Android asks you to allow installs from your browser: tap Settings, switch on Allow from this source, and go back. This is a one-time step.
  4. Install & open — tap Install, then open GPS Rocket Locator ’26. Grant the Bluetooth (“Nearby devices”) and Location permissions when asked — Bluetooth talks to your receiver, Location lets the app navigate you to the rocket.

Updating: download the new APK from the same button and install it over the old one — settings and data are kept. Prefer automatic updates? Install Obtainium and add this app’s GitHub page; it will fetch each new release for you.

Beta status

The app is in active development (version 0.NN series) and being field-tested with real Eggfinder hardware. It’s an homage to Brune Studios’ original GPS Rocket Locator (2013–2020), rebuilt from scratch for modern Android. Bug reports and flight logs from your launches genuinely shape what gets built next.
